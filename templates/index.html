📖 Reading the Scanner
Score

A composite 0-100 number combining all the columns below. Higher = more scalp-worthy. The formula weights Volume Ratio (30%), ATR% (30%), |Gap%| (20%), and Range% (20%).

60+ Hot — high activity 35-59 Moderate <35 Low activity
Chg% — Change Percent

How much the price moved from yesterday's close to right now. Shows current momentum direction. Green = up, Red = down. Large Chg% means the stock is moving today.

Gap% — Overnight Gap

The difference between today's open and yesterday's close. A stock that closed at $100 and opened at $103 has a +3% gap. Big gaps usually mean news, earnings, or a catalyst happened overnight. Scalpers love gap stocks because they trend and have high volume at open.

  • ±1-2% — Normal, mild interest
  • ±2-5% — Notable catalyst, expect strong volume
  • ±5%+ — Major event (earnings, FDA, etc.)
Vol Ratio — Volume Ratio

Today's volume divided by the 20-day average volume. This is arguably the most important column for scalping. It tells you if more people than usual are trading this stock right now.

  • 1-1.5x — Normal day
  • 1.5-3x — Above average interest
  • 3-5x — Unusual volume, something is happening
  • 5x+ — Massive volume spike — news, earnings, or short squeeze

The bar next to the number is a visual indicator (fills up to 5x).

ATR% — Average True Range

The stock's average daily price swing over the past 14 days, expressed as a percentage of the price. This tells you how much a stock typically moves in a single day.

Example: A $50 stock with ATR% = 4% typically swings $2/day. That's your profit potential per trade.

  • <1% — Barely moves. Skip for scalping.
  • 1-2% — Moderate, fine for large positions
  • 2-4% — Good scalp range
  • 4%+ — Very volatile, bigger moves but riskier
Range% — Intraday Range

How much the stock has moved today specifically: (Day High - Day Low) / Open × 100. Compare this to ATR% — if Range% is already near ATR%, the stock may have used up most of its daily move. If Range% is low but ATR% is high, there may still be a move coming.

📚 Trading Glossary

Scalping
Making many small, quick trades — holding for seconds to minutes. You profit from small price movements, not big swings. Requires liquid, volatile stocks.
Catalyst
An event that causes unusual price movement — earnings reports, FDA approvals, analyst upgrades, macro news, CEO tweets. Stocks with catalysts have the volume and momentum scalpers need.
Risk/Reward Ratio
How much you stand to gain vs how much you could lose. A 2:1 ratio means your profit target is 2x your stop loss. Good scalpers aim for at least 1.5:1 — even with a 50% win rate, you'll be profitable.
PDT Rule (Pattern Day Trader)
SEC rule requiring $25,000 minimum account balance to make 4+ day trades in 5 business days (in a margin account). Below $25K, you're limited to 3 day trades per 5 days. Cash accounts are exempt but require settled funds.

VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)
The average price a stock has traded at all day, weighted by volume. Institutional traders use VWAP as a benchmark. Price above VWAP = bullish, below = bearish. Many scalpers buy pullbacks to VWAP in an uptrend.
HOD / LOD
High of Day / Low of Day. Key levels — breaking above HOD signals strength, breaking below LOD signals weakness. Many scalpers buy HOD breakouts or short LOD breakdowns.
Gap Up / Gap Down
When a stock opens significantly higher (gap up) or lower (gap down) than yesterday's close. Caused by overnight news. Gap and go = stock gaps up and keeps running. Gap fill = stock gaps up then falls back to yesterday's close.
Volume Profile
Shows how much volume traded at each price level. High-volume price levels act as support/resistance. Scalpers look for stocks trading above a high-volume node for bullish setups.

Float
The number of shares available for public trading. Low float (<20M shares) stocks move faster because there are fewer shares to buy/sell. High volume + low float = explosive moves.
Short Interest
Percentage of float currently sold short (betting the price goes down). High short interest (20%+) means potential for a short squeeze if the stock starts rising.
Short Squeeze
When a heavily shorted stock rises, forcing short sellers to buy shares to cover. This buying pressure pushes the price even higher in a feedback loop. GameStop (GME) in 2021 was the famous example.
Premarket / After Hours
Trading outside regular hours (9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET). Premarket runs 4 AM - 9:30 AM ET. Volume is lower and spreads are wider, but this is where gaps form.

Bid / Ask Spread
The difference between the highest price a buyer will pay (bid) and the lowest a seller will accept (ask). Tight spread ($0.01) = liquid, easy to trade. Wide spread ($0.10+) = illiquid, costs you on every trade.
Level 2 / Order Book
Shows all pending buy and sell orders at each price level. Scalpers use it to see where big orders sit (support/resistance) and gauge supply vs demand in real-time.
Tape Reading
Watching the Time & Sales window to see real-time individual trades. Large block prints (10K+ shares) at the ask = institutional buying. Useful for confirming momentum.
Slippage
The difference between the price you expected and the price you actually got. Happens in fast-moving stocks. Use limit orders instead of market orders to control slippage.
